Light and Photosynthesis in the Cell

How a plant cell turns sunlight into sugar, and why the colour of light matters.

1. The big idea

Photosynthesis is how plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to build sugar from carbon dioxide and water. In plant cells it happens inside green organelles called chloroplasts.

Light energy is stored as chemical energy in glucose, and oxygen is released as a by-product.

6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy → C6H12O6 + 6 O2

2. Where it happens: the chloroplast

A chloroplast is usually 5-10 µm long, and one leaf cell can hold dozens. It has a double membrane. Inside is a fluid called the stroma and flat sacs called thylakoids, stacked like coins into grana.

Light reactions occur on the thylakoid membranes. The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma.

3. Light and pigments

Plants use visible light, about 400-700 nm. Chlorophylls absorb mostly blue-violet and red light. Green light (500-600 nm) is mostly reflected, which is why leaves look green.

Shorter wavelengths carry more energy per photon, but a pigment can only use light that matches what it can absorb.

PigmentMain absorptionRole
Chlorophyll a~430 and ~662 nmMain pigment; reaction centres
Chlorophyll b~453 and ~642 nmAntenna; passes energy to chlorophyll a
Carotenoids~450-500 nmAntenna; protect from excess light

Fun fact: Leaves turn yellow in autumn because chlorophyll breaks down first, revealing carotenoids.

4. Stage 1: the light reactions

These occur in the thylakoid membrane. Photosystems capture light and pass the energy to a special chlorophyll a pair: P680 (Photosystem II) and P700 (Photosystem I).

  1. Photosystem II absorbs light and an excited electron leaves P680.
  2. Water is split: 2 H2O → O2 + 4 H+ + 4 e-. The electrons replace those lost, and O2 is released.
  3. Electron transport chain: electrons move downhill and pump H+ into the lumen.
  4. Photosystem I re-energises the electrons at P700.
  5. NADPH is made when NADP+ is reduced.
  6. ATP is made as H+ flows through ATP synthase (chemiosmosis).

5. Stage 2: the Calvin cycle

It does not use light directly, but depends on the ATP and NADPH from the light reactions. It runs in the stroma:

  • Fixation: RuBisCO attaches CO2 to a 5-carbon sugar, RuBP.
  • Reduction: ATP and NADPH convert the product into G3P, a 3-carbon sugar.
  • Regeneration: most G3P is rebuilt into RuBP so the cycle continues.

6. How light affects the rate

At low light, the rate rises steeply because more light means more ATP and NADPH. At the light saturation point the curve levels off, because another factor (CO2, temperature or enzymes) becomes the limit.

FactorEffect
Light intensityRaises rate until saturation; very strong light can damage photosystems
WavelengthRed and blue work best; green least
CO2More CO2 raises rate until another factor limits
TemperatureEnzymes speed up when warm, denature when too hot

7. Summary

  • Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts; light is captured on thylakoid membranes.
  • Chlorophylls absorb mainly blue and red light.
  • Light reactions: light + water → ATP + NADPH + O2.
  • Calvin cycle: CO2 + ATP + NADPH → sugar.
  • Rate rises with light, then plateaus.
